# Martian Surface Radiation: GCR Doses, Quality Factors, and Gale Reference Limits

## 1. Baseline Radiation Environment
Curiosity's RAD instrument provided the first comprehensive in-situ measurements of the ionizing radiation environment on the Martian surface:
- **Galactic Cosmic Ray (GCR) Dose Rate:** Approximately 0.21 mGy/day absorbed dose in silicon / tissue-equivalent detector material during solar minimum conditions.
- **Equivalent Dose Rate:** Incorporating high-LET particles (protons, alpha particles, heavy ions, and secondary neutrons produced in the Martian regolith and atmosphere), the mean radiation quality factor $Q \approx 3.05$.
- **Equivalent Dose:** Approximately 0.64 mSv/day (millisieverts per day) under nominal atmospheric shielding conditions.

## 2. Atmospheric Column Shielding & Elevation Dependence
- Mars lacks an intrinsic global dipole magnetic field; the atmosphere serves as the primary radiation shield (areal density ~18 to 22 g/cm² at crater floor levels).
- Atmospheric column mass fluctuates seasonally by ~25% due to the sublimation and condensation of polar CO2 ice caps.
- Elevated topography experiences reduced atmospheric shielding, resulting in slightly elevated GCR dose rates (+5% to +10% along elevated crater rims relative to deep basin floors).

## 3. Transferability Constraints from Gale to Jezero
- **Reference Nature:** The 0.21 mGy/day value was directly measured at Gale Crater (-4.5°S, elevation -4400m).
- **Jezero Crater Comparison:** Jezero Crater is located at 18.38°N with a floor elevation of approximately -2500m (roughly 1900m higher than Gale's floor). 
- **Scientific Limitation:** While 0.21 mGy/day is the standard NASA mission planning reference, the higher elevation of Jezero slightly reduces total atmospheric column shielding, introducing an estimated ±10% systematic uncertainty.
