# Jezero Crater Floor and Margin Lithologies: Scientific Priorities

## 1. Crater Floor Geologic Units
The Jezero crater floor is composed of two primary lithological domains:
1. **The Máaz Formation:** An upper, crater-retaining lava flow or volcaniclastic package of basaltic-andesite composition. It presents a relatively flat, blocky pavement with average slopes < 4°, allowing predictable and low-resistance surface locomotion.
2. **The Séítah Formation:** An underlying, layered olivine-cumulate igneous unit with extensive carbonate and sulfate alteration. Séítah exposures are characterized by elevated bedrock ridges separated by aeolian bedforms (transverse aeolian ridges / TARs).

## 2. Terrain Hazards & Sand Ripple Traversability
- While the bedrock ridges of Séítah offer solid footing, the intervening depressions frequently trap modern active sand ripples and drifts.
- Active Martian sand drifts exhibit low bearing capacity. Suited crew or wheeled vehicles risk sinkage exceeding 15 cm in unconsolidated windblown dust pockets.
- Mission planning rules designate large aeolian ripple fields (> 0.5 m crest height) as terrain exclusion zones, requiring circumnavigation along exposed bedrock margins.
