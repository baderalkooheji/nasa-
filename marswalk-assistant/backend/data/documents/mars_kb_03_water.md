# Water recovery and resource use

## Why water is hard to obtain on Mars
Very low atmospheric pressure makes liquid water unstable on the surface. Subsurface ice is a potential resource, but whether it
is accessible varies by location.

## Subsurface water ice maps (SWIM)
NASA's Subsurface Water Ice Mapping (SWIM) project combines orbital observations to identify areas where shallow subsurface ice may
be present. Ice could support drinking water and, after processing, other mission needs. Map indications are estimates and do not
guarantee an accessible deposit at a particular landing site. Ice is not confirmed at a selected coordinate unless a suitable
dataset supports it; this project does not include the SWIM ice dataset.

## Water recovery demonstrated on the ISS (status: demonstrated on the ISS, not on Mars)
NASA's Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) on the International Space Station recovers water from wastewater,
urine and cabin humidity. In 2023 NASA reported a 98% water-recovery milestone on the ISS after adding the Brine Processor
Assembly. This is an ISS demonstration; it is not a system operating on Mars.
