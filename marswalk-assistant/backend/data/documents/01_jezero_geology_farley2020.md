# Jezero Crater Delta Front: Stratigraphy and Depositional Environments

## 1. Geological Overview
Jezero crater is a 45-km-diameter Noachian impact basin located at 18.38°N, 77.58°E along the western margin of Isidis Planitia. The western delta deposit preserves prominent bottomset, foreset, and topset stratigraphic architectures formed during an open-basin lacustrine epoch.

## 2. Delta Scarp Geomorphology & Traversability
The delta scarp exposes prominent boulder-bearing conglomerate horizons overlying fine-grained lacustrine mudstones. 
- Slope angles at the delta front range from 12° to over 25° along erosional scarps (e.g., Kodiak butte and the main delta scarp).
- Slopes in excess of 15° frequently consist of unconsolidated talus and scree composed of cobble-to-boulder sized basaltic fragments embedded in poorly sorted regolith.
- For exploratory mobility, regolith repose angles on Mars hover around 20° to 30°. Traversal on loose scarp faces exceeding 15° involves severe slip, potential avalanching of scree, and loss of foot or wheel traction.
- Bottomset beds at the base of the delta scarp transition smoothly into crater floor units (Séítah and Máaz formations), providing gentle gradients (< 5°) ideal for horizontal traversability.

## 3. Provenance and Lithological Variations
The lower delta sequence (Shenandoah formation) contains high concentrations of smectite clays and phyllosilicates capable of preserving biosignatures. Higher stratigraphic layers represent high-energy fluvial flood stages characterized by massive boulder beds.
