# Protecting crews from radiation

## Why radiation is a problem on Mars
Mars lacks Earth's global magnetic field and has a thin atmosphere, so the surface and the journey to Mars are exposed to galactic
cosmic rays and solar particle events.

## Measuring it: Curiosity RAD (status: demonstrated)
NASA's Curiosity rover carries the Radiation Assessment Detector (RAD), which measured radiation on the way to Mars and at the
surface (Gale Crater). These measurements help estimate astronaut exposure and inform shielding requirements.

## How to protect astronauts: shielding options (status: proposed, need mission-specific design)
Potential shielding approaches include using the habitat structure, stored supplies or local regolith (for example buried or
regolith-covered shelters and storm shelters). The actual protection depends on the material, thickness, geometry and the type of
radiation, so shielding design needs mission-specific analysis.
