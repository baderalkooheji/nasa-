# Key Martian planetary data

Values can vary slightly by reference definition, location and season.

## Surface gravity
Mars surface gravity is 3.71 m/s², about 38% of Earth's (Earth: 9.81 m/s²).

## Day length (sol)
One Martian day, a sol, lasts about 24 h 39 min 35 s. An Earth day is about 24 hours, so a sol is roughly 40 minutes longer.

## Year length
A Mars year lasts 687 Earth days, about 669 Martian sols (Earth: 365.25 days).

## Distance from the Sun
Mars orbits at an average of about 228 million km from the Sun (1.52 AU). Earth: about 150 million km (1 AU).

## Atmosphere composition (what the air is made of)
The Martian atmosphere is made of about 95.1% carbon dioxide (CO2), 2.6% nitrogen (N2), 1.9% argon and 0.16% oxygen.
Earth's atmosphere is about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen and about 1% other gases.

## Surface pressure
The atmosphere is very thin: surface pressure is around 610–636 Pa at commonly cited reference conditions and varies by place and season.
Earth sea-level pressure is about 101,325 Pa. The often-quoted 610 Pa is a reasonable approximation, not a universal fixed value;
NASA's Mars fact sheet reports pressure that changes with season and location.

## Average temperature
The average temperature on Mars is roughly −60 °C, with large local and seasonal variation (Earth global average: about +15 °C).

## What these numbers mean for exploration
Mars has much weaker gravity, a very thin carbon-dioxide-rich atmosphere, and a longer day and year than Earth. These conditions
affect human health, surface operations, energy systems and mission planning. These are global values: they do not by themselves
describe conditions at Jezero Crater or at a point selected on the map.
